# Video Submission

> SAUVC 2026 requires every team to submit a 30-second video prior to the competition. This document describes what the team produced, what it demonstrated, and what the review committee assessed.

---

## Why a video submission

SAUVC uses a video qualification stage to filter entries before the in-person competition. Every registered team must submit a short video; the organising committee reviews these and shortlists around **35 teams** to attend the event in Singapore.

The video submission is not optional and is not just marketing — it is the formal gate to the competition. Teams that submit a video that does not meet the stated criteria are not considered.

---

## What the rulebook requires

From the SAUVC 2026 rulebook, the submitted video **has to**:

- Be **maximum 30 seconds long**.
- Be submitted **before the deadline** (23:59, 4 January 2026, Anywhere On Earth).
- Show **tether-less operation** of the AUV swimming underwater for **at least 10 seconds**.
- **Clearly show** that pressing the **kill switch** stops all thrusters immediately.

The rulebook is explicit that reviewers will not play the video beyond the 30-second mark, and will not review anything beyond that window. So the safety and control demonstration — the parts that prove the AUV is competition-legal — had to appear **inside** the 30 seconds, not after.

The rulebook also states:

> Do not put the most important parts of the video after 30 seconds (safety, control, etc.), as it will not be reviewed, without exception.
>
> You do not need to showcase an intro of team structure, university details etc. — this will only take up valuable time in your video.

This meant no team logo, no university name, no long intro. The whole 30 seconds had to be the AUV, the water, and the kill switch demonstration.

---

## What the team submitted

A 30-second underwater video of the AUV swimming in a test pool. The video showed:

- The AUV operating **untethered**, fully submerged.
- Continuous underwater swimming for well over the required 10 seconds.
- The **kill switch pressed mid-run**, with all thrusters stopping immediately.
- A clear view of the vehicle's overall form and layout, consistent with the CAD assembly in the team repository.

The AUV swam under its own power with no physical connection to the surface during the run.

---

## What the review committee was looking for

Based on the rulebook and the way SAUVC has run previous years, the review committee assesses whether:

1. The video meets the **hard technical criteria** (30 s, untethered, ≥ 10 s underwater, kill switch demonstrated).
2. The AUV is a **plausible competition vehicle** — size, form factor, and safety features consistent with the rules.
3. The video was clearly **submitted by the deadline** and follows the format requirements.

Videos are reviewed **as a whole**; there is no partial credit for meeting some criteria and not others. If any of the four required elements is missing, the video is not accepted.

---

## Outcome

Team Diyakawa's video submission was **accepted**, and the team was **shortlisted** to attend SAUVC 2026.

Travel to Singapore was not possible for the team in 2026, so the competition was not attended in person.

---


## Why this matters for the project

For a portfolio, the video does several things that a written description cannot:

- It **proves** the AUV actually swam underwater, under its own power, without a tether — not a render or a bench test.
- It demonstrates the **kill switch** — a specific safety requirement, not just a feature.
- It shows the real physical vehicle, complementing the CAD assembly that lives in the team repository.

Anyone reviewing this repo can watch the 30 seconds and see, at a glance, that the team built and tested a working untethered underwater vehicle.
